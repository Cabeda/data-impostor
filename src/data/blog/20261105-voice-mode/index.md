Last week I've gotten access to a codex subscription. One of the features I was most curious to try out was it's voice chat mode. At first I didn't understand why some people were so hyped about it aside from the cool demo they showed on [Astra's release](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1QNsdr-Qx_I).

However, I had a [decision model demo](https://github.com/Cabeda/decision-models-demo) I was postponing to prepare and I thought I could give it a go. 

I had the demmo already prepared using kiro and I knew the jist of what was done because I had asked for it. 

1. I wanted to present what jev and decision models were using the docs
2. Show 3/4 use cases
3. Run it locally (asked to use ollaya)
4. Had to be shown in 25 minutes

However to do an actual presentation it's important to 1) understand the content and 2) train it so that it goes without any hiccups.

As I was postponing the actual training of the presentation I clicked on the voice chat inside my demo folder and prompted with:

> I have to present the demo, set a timer, listen to me present and feel free to give me notes

And that was it. With a calmly presence doing it's occasional "hmm" I suddenly had a companion that was listening to me, transcribing what I said and after ~20 minutes I got a really useful report on what chatgpt felt I could improve. 

In ~40 minutes I was able to enter the flow, get focused on a single task (something I've been struggling to do with the advent of the coding agents) and deliver a presentation that I felt was at the required level for the audience.
